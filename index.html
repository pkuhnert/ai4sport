Overview

With the 2032 Olympic and Paralympic Games set to be held in Brisbane, Australia, there is a timely opportunity to advance trustworthy AI methods that support athlete performance, safety, wellbeing, and inclusive participation across sport ecosystems. While Olympic and Paralympic sports provide a globally visible catalyst, the challenges addressed in this workshop extend broadly across professional, community, adaptive, and emerging digital sports contexts, including Australian football, cricket, rugby, women’s sport, and e-sports.

This workshop brings together researchers and practitioners working at the intersection of machine learning, statistics, sports science, biomechanics, human performance, and applied decision-making. Sport provides a uniquely demanding real-world environment for AI, requiring methods that can operate under uncertainty, integrate multimodal data streams, support embodied and team-based interaction, and remain interpretable, ethical, and robust in high-stakes human settings.

Themes

Multi-agent Learning in Team Sports

Team sports provide a natural testbed for multi-agent learning, sequential decision-making and spatio-temporal modelling Decroos et al. (2019). Athlete performance and injury risk are shaped not only by individual capability, but by interactions among teammates and opponents, tactical systems, game state, coaching decisions and competition demands. This theme invites work on graph learning, reinforcement learning, counterfactual reasoning, and dynamic workload modelling that supports performance, safety and decision-making in complex team environments.
