Workshop Details

Overview

The arrival of NeurIPS 2026 in Sydney, followed by the Brisbane 2032 Olympic and Paralympic Games, creates a unique runway for connecting frontier machine learning research with real-world challenges in sport. This workshop will harness that momentum to advance trustworthy AI methods that support athlete performance, safety, wellbeing, and inclusive participation across professional, community, adaptive, women’s and emerging digital sports contexts, including e-sports.

Sport provides a uniquely demanding real-world environment for AI, requiring methods that can operate under uncertainty, integrate multimodal data streams, support embodied and team-based interaction, and remain interpretable, ethical, and robust in high-stakes human settings.

This workshop brings together researchers and practitioners working at the intersection of machine learning (ML), statistics, sports science, biomechanics, human performance, and applied decision-making.

Themes

Workshop Themes

The workshop will focus on four themes that encourage technical development, critical reflection, and theory-informed practice. Across all themes, we will emphasise co-designed approaches that integrate technical advances with socio-technical considerations, including governance, privacy, athlete wellbeing, stakeholder perspectives, uncertainty communication and responsible deployment.

Theme 1

Multi-agent Learning and Multimodal Intelligence in Team Sports

Team sports provide a natural testbed for multi-agent learning, sequential decision-making, causal reasoning and spatio-temporal modelling (Decroos et al. 2019). This theme explores how AI systems can learn meaningful abstractions of game style and structure from multi-modal data, including video, positional data, event streams, match statistics, wearable sensors, biomechanical signals, medical and wellness data, coaching notes and commentary. Relevant methods include representation learning, unsupervised phase-of-play discovery, multimodal game-state embeddings, event detection, player-role inference, reinforcement-learning and agent-based simulations and causal inference for rule changes.

Theme 2

Advancing Participation, Performance and Safety in Paralympic sports

Paralympic sports provide a critical testbed for inclusive, data-scarce sports AI. Compared with many able-bodied sport settings, data are often less systematically collected, cohorts are smaller, and athlete trajectories may be shaped by heterogeneous impairments, assistive technologies, wheelchair or prosthetic equipment, sport-specific classification systems and highly individualised biomechanics (Liu et al. 2026, Rum et al. 2025). This theme focuses on how statistical ML methods can support Para-athlete performance while respecting privacy, dignity and fairness.

Theme 3

Responsible AI for Sport and Human Performance

This theme focuses on the responsible evaluation and deployment of sports AI systems in settings where data are sensitive, proprietary, non-standardised and highly context dependent (Davis et al. 2024; Salehi et al. 2024). We invite work on privacy-preserving and synthetic data, federated learning, benchmarking, external validation, reporting standards, athlete data rights, consent and contestability, and human oversight and audit trails. Contributions may address athlete performance, injury risk, selection, officiating, fan engagement or organisational decision-making.

Theme 4

The Future of Sports Data Science Towards 2032

Sports data science is entering a new phase, moving from retrospective analytics and isolated prediction tools toward real-time, multimodal and autonomous decision-support systems. contributions on agentic AI, computer vision, wearable and sensor-based analytics, digital twins, synthetic data, e-sports, automated broadcasting, fan intelligence, officiating support and AI-enabled sport operations. Looking toward 2032 as a horizon for the next generation of sport technology, the session asks how data science can support performance, safety, inclusion, integrity and engagement while addressing emerging risks around privacy, bias, accessibility, explainability, governance and human oversight. The theme draws on future-facing work including the IOC’s Olympic AI Agenda (IOC 2024), sport-AI foresight reports (Bratanova et al. 2025), and recent academic reviews of AI (Zhou et al. 2025; Vec et l. 2024) , analytics and real-time methods in sport.

Call For Papers

Submission information

We invite original research related to diverse areas such as computer vision, multi-modal language models, reinforcement learning, functional data analysis and probabilistic methods. In addition, socio-technical aspects of AI and general ML approaches, such as supervised and unsupervised will be considered.

  • Research papers: 4 to 9 pages, unlimited references and appendices.
  • Accepted papers will be presented in a poster session in addition to being alotted a 1 minute lightning talk.
  • Submissions are reviewed in a double-blind process.
