Workshop Details

Overview

The arrival of NeurIPS 2026 in Sydney, along with the emergence of key international conferences on sports analytics (ASA Sports Analytics Conference, International Sports Analytics Conference and Exhibition (ISACE)), followed by the Brisbane 2032 Olympic and Paralympic Games, creates a unique runway for connecting machine learning (ML) research with real-world challenges in sport. A key focus of this workshop is the new and exciting ML and AI methods developed by the NeurIPS community that can help address some of the complex challenges facing sport and human performance.

Some open-ended problems this workshop will touch on are:

  • Recent data-driven AI methods for team sports include multi-agent learning, sequential decision-making, causal reasoning and spatio-temporal modelling. Coupled with the use of generative AI to interpret video play poses interesting questions like, can generative AI assist in game play that is tracked from broadcast footage, and can this type of information allow a coach to make near real-time decisions on player positions and strategy?
  • Paralympic sports have had a focus on sport-specific classification systems using ML and biomechanics. Compared with many able-bodied sport settings, data are often less systematically collected and individualised to the athlete, making traditional ML method development challenging. In light of this, how can we develop ML and AI methods that make use of mobile video to improve the classification of Para athletes and provide individualised improvements to their sport?
  • Responsible AI (RAI) for sport as highlighted in a recent report presents ongoing challenges in methodology development due to the nature of data collection in this space, i.e. data are sensitive, proprietary, non-standardised and highly context dependent. How can we incorporate and operationalise RAI practices into the ML and AI methods that are being developed?
  • As virtual reality is becoming more widely accessible, how might ML/AI methods be developed to generate a more personalised training program for sports professionals through this medium?
  • As esports continue to mature as professional sports, how can ML and AI methods evolve to provide a competitive edge in these environments?

This workshop provides a unique offering, bringing together researchers and practitioners working at the intersection of ML, statistics, sports science, biomechanics, human performance, and applied decision-making. Methods of interest will be diverse and include areas such as computer vision, multi-modal language models, reinforcement learning, functional data analysis and probabilistic methods along with socio-technical aspects of AI, responsible AI practices and general ML approaches, such as supervised and unsupervised learning.

Themes

Workshop Themes

The workshop will focus on four themes that encourage technical development, critical reflection, and theory-informed practice. Across all themes, we will emphasise co-designed approaches that integrate technical advances with socio-technical considerations, including governance, privacy, athlete wellbeing, stakeholder perspectives, uncertainty communication and responsible deployment.

Theme 1

Multi-agent Learning and Multimodal Intelligence in Team Sports

Team sports provide a natural testbed for multi-agent learning, sequential decision-making, causal reasoning and spatio-temporal modelling. This theme explores how AI systems can learn meaningful abstractions of game style and structure from multi-modal data, including video, positional data, event streams, match statistics, wearable sensors, biomechanical signals, medical and wellness data, coaching notes and commentary. Relevant methods include representation learning, unsupervised phase-of-play discovery, multimodal game-state embeddings, event detection, player-role inference, reinforcement-learning and agent-based simulations and causal inference for rule changes.

Theme 2

Advancing Participation, Performance and Safety in Paralympic sports

Paralympic sports provide a critical testbed for inclusive, data-scarce sports AI. Compared with many able-bodied sport settings, data are often less systematically collected, cohorts are smaller, and athlete trajectories may be shaped by heterogeneous impairments, assistive technologies, wheelchair or prosthetic equipment, sport-specific classification systems and highly individualised biomechanics. This theme focuses on how statistical ML methods can support Para-athlete performance while respecting privacy, dignity and fairness.

Theme 3

Responsible AI for Sport and Human Performance

This theme focuses on the responsible evaluation and deployment of sports AI systems in settings where data are sensitive, proprietary, non-standardised and highly context dependent. We invite work on privacy-preserving and synthetic data, federated learning, benchmarking, external validation, reporting standards, athlete data rights, consent and contestability, and human oversight and audit trails. Contributions may address athlete performance, injury risk, selection, officiating, fan engagement or organisational decision-making.

Theme 4

The Future of Sports Data Science Towards 2032

Sports data science is entering a new phase, moving from retrospective analytics and isolated prediction tools toward real-time, multimodal and autonomous decision-support systems. contributions on agentic AI, computer vision, wearable and sensor-based analytics, digital twins, synthetic data, e-sports, automated broadcasting, fan intelligence, officiating support and AI-enabled sport operations. Looking toward 2032 as a horizon for the next generation of sport technology, the session asks how data science can support performance, safety, inclusion, integrity and engagement while addressing emerging risks around privacy, bias, accessibility, explainability, governance and human oversight. The theme draws on future-facing work including the IOC’s Olympic AI Agenda, the sport-AI foresight report, and the Responsible AI in sport report.

Call For Papers

Submission information

We invite original research related to diverse areas such as computer vision, multi-modal language models, reinforcement learning, functional data analysis and probabilistic methods. In addition, socio-technical aspects of AI and general ML approaches, such as supervised and unsupervised will be considered.

  • Research papers: 4 pages, unlimited references and appendices.
  • Accepted papers will be presented in a poster session in addition to being alotted a 1 minute lightning talk.
  • Submissions are reviewed in a double-blind process.
